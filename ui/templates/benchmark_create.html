New Benchmark

A benchmark is a tournament-style elimination of a large number of models; giving them a small sample of tasks to complete in randomized, 1:1 match-ups.

{# ── Intro card: three-column feature highlights ─────── #}
help_outline

What problem do benchmarks help solve?

If you've made a test, but have no idea what models might actually score well against it, benchmarks are a great place to start.

Benchmarks pit small sample sizes against a large number of models to give a general idea of performance at a given task.

account_tree

How do benchmarks work?

Each benchmark match-up pits 2 models against each other to see which one gets a higher F1 score when scored against a small input sample of a larger test.

Each match is a different random sample and provides broad coverage across the whole benchmark process (e.g. you can quickly see if LLMs are good at the task at all).

lightbulb

Things to keep in mind

1. The larger the sample size, the more expensive the benchmark will be.

2. Benchmarks are directional, not authoritative, when it comes to quality.

3. Each match is its own random sample, and model match-ups are chosen at random. This adds a level of chance to the whole process; strong models can be eliminated up front, some rounds may be genuinely harder samples then others.
